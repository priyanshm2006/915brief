India Post-Close Wrap

Private analytical edition · Nine Fifteen desk
Tuesday, 25 August 2026
Compiled 17:45 IST · monthly F&O expiry session
What happened

A weak open reversed. The Nifty was down to 24,115.45 before turning around 2 p.m. and closing at the day's high, 24,334.55 (+0.48%); the Sensex traced the same path from 77,125.91 to 77,656.09 (+0.37%). The trigger was oil. Scott Bessent's Iran sanctions package, announced Monday, landed without effective dates, wind-down periods or a timeline for secondary sanctions on China — and Brent fell roughly 3% to about $89, having settled near $92 on Monday. For a crude importer with an RBI that turned hawkish in its 19 August minutes, that is the whole chain: cheaper oil, easier bond math, a bid under equities.

What it means

Read the internals before the headline. Breadth was negative — 2,105 advances against 2,218 declines on the BSE — and the Nifty Smallcap 100 finished down about 0.1%. This was a narrow, large-cap, expiry-day recovery, not broad accumulation. Bank Nifty went nowhere (57,514.20, −0.02%) as PSU banks rose and private banks fell; the leadership sat in healthcare, pharma and consumer durables. India VIX at 11.08 (−3.9%) says the options market priced the sanctions headline as noise, not regime change.

What to watch

Two hard catalysts, both offshore. US July PCE and the second Q2 GDP estimate print 18:00 IST Wednesday; Nvidia reports after the US close, roughly 01:50 IST Thursday. Then Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as Fed Chair, 19:30 IST Friday, nineteen days before the 16 September FOMC. Domestically the September series starts from a clean slate after today's settlement, and the Hindustan Copper OFS retail tranche opens tomorrow at a ₹514 floor. Today's FII/DII cash split had not been published at the time of writing — it is the first thing to check tonight.

Regulatory

SEBI drops the Max–Axis case

All proceedings dismissed against Max Financial, Axis Max Life, Axis Bank, Axis Capital, Axis Securities and seven individuals including Analjit Singh, over 2010/2015/2020 arrangements alleged to have cost shareholders ₹3,911.95 cr.
