Post-Close Wrap

Tuesday, 25 August 2026

What happened today, and what it sets up for tomorrow.

Today's Story

What happened. Indian equities opened weak, slipped to session lows by late morning, then reversed to close at the day's highs — the Nifty 50 up 0.48% at 24,334.55 and the Sensex up 0.37% at 77,656.09, on today's monthly futures-and-options expiry. The turn traced back to crude: Brent fell roughly 3% after the US Treasury's new Iran sanctions package left enforcement dates and a trailed China secondary-sanctions timeline unspecified, and markets read that ambiguity as delay rather than escalation. That also reversed Monday's sector pattern — PSU banks, which had fallen on rising bond yields, led today's gainers, while metals, yesterday's leaders, faded.

How this reaches India: India imports the large majority of the crude it consumes, so a lower oil price works through the economy along a fairly direct chain: it shrinks the import bill, which eases pressure on the current account and the rupee, and it reduces the inflation pass-through that has been the Reserve Bank's stated concern since its 19 August minutes, in which Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta raised the prospect of a rate increase later this year and Governor Sanjay Malhotra said inflation spillovers into food, fuel and input costs "could justify policy tightening." That concern had already pushed the 10-year government bond yield to an eight-week high of 6.86–6.87% and had hit PSU banks' mark-to-market book yesterday. Cheaper crude loosens that chain at its first link — which is the mechanical reason the same PSU banks that fell on yield fears yesterday led today's rally, and why bond yields are the number to watch next.

Overnight, watch US Case-Shiller/FHFA housing data, Conference Board consumer confidence and a two-year Treasury auction. Wednesday brings India's M3 money-supply print alongside the week's real fulcrum — US July PCE inflation, the second estimate of Q2 GDP and Nvidia's earnings after the US close — with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as the week's close on Friday.

Macro & Policy

RBI policy stance

The repo rate is unchanged at 5.25% with a neutral stance, but the 19 August minutes flagged risk: Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta raised the prospect of a rate increase later this year, and Governor Sanjay Malhotra said inflation spillovers into food, fuel and input costs "could justify policy tightening." The 10-year government bond yield had climbed to an eight-week high of 6.86–6.87% on that concern before today's fall in crude.

RBI, 19 Aug minutes; yield as of 24 Aug

Fed funds rate and long-end yields

The federal funds target range is 3.50%–3.75%, effective 30 July 2026. The 30-year Treasury yield read 5.24% today, described by one data provider as the highest since 2007 — a more consequential figure for equities than the 10-year, since long-end yields set the discount rate used to value future earnings.

Federal Reserve implementation note; single-provider figure for the 30-year

US PCE inflation due Wednesday

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge — July PCE and core PCE — is due at 18:00 IST Wednesday alongside the second estimate of Q2 GDP, durable goods orders and personal income and spending. Consensus sits at 3.7% and 3.3% year-on-year for the two measures; consensus and forecast sources disagree on the core PCE monthly figure (0.1% versus 0.3%), so that number should be read with care.

Trading Economics, InvestingLive

Tariff sequence on Indian goods

The widely repeated "18%" reciprocal tariff figure from February 2026 was superseded when the US Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on 20 February. A Section 122 surcharge followed and sunset on 24 July 2026, replaced the same day by a Section 301 action under which India sits on a 10% tier, with MFN base duties applying on top. This sequence is corroborated by two independent trade-policy sources but not against a USTR, Federal Register or CBP notice, so the specific tier should be treated as probable rather than confirmed. No India–US tariff news was reported today.

India Briefing, WilmerHale, White & Case, TariffsTool

Germany beats, India quiet

No Indian macroeconomic data was scheduled today. Germany's final Q2 GDP came in at +0.3% quarter-on-quarter versus a +0.2% preliminary reading, and the ifo business climate index for August rose to 88.8 against expectations of 87.2 and a prior 86.6 — a beat that helped European sentiment.

ifo Institute, Business Standard

Jackson Hole ahead

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers the Jackson Hole keynote at 19:30 IST Friday — his first as Chair, nineteen days before the 16 September FOMC meeting. The symposium runs 27–29 August on "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." The keynote time is reported by two sources but had not been officially confirmed by the Kansas City Fed at the time of writing.

Kiplinger, The Front Month (unconfirmed by Kansas City Fed)

Corporate & Primary Market

TCS to acquire Porsche's IT arm

TCS's board approved acquiring 100% of MHP Management- und IT-Beratung GmbH, Porsche's IT subsidiary, at an enterprise value of €320 million (about ₹3,574 crore), within a five-year, €1.25 billion strategic partnership with Porsche. Announced Monday evening, the stock reversed early gains to close down about 1%.

Company announcement, Monday evening

Federal Bank denies Jana stake talk

Federal Bank denied reports that it was acquiring a controlling stake in Jana Small Finance Bank, stating there was "no material event or information that requires disclosure." Federal Bank fell as much as 4% intraday to close at ₹347.05, down about 3%; Jana Small Finance Bank fell 2.1% to ₹569.20.

Company disclosure

SEBI drops Max Financial proceedings

SEBI dropped all proceedings against Max Financial Services, Axis Max Life, Axis Bank, Axis Capital, Axis Securities and seven individuals including Analjit Singh, over arrangements dating to 2010, 2015 and 2020 alleged to have cost shareholders ₹3,911.95 crore. The regulator found the disclosure charges unsustainable and no fraudulent scheme. Settlement applications from thirteen independent directors remain in abeyance.

SEBI order

A heavy day for capital raising

Piramal Finance opened a QIP at a floor price of ₹2,102.65 targeting ₹2,100 crore. ICICI Bank is pricing $1 billion of senior unsecured notes; Kotak Mahindra Bank allotted $650 million of 5.478% notes due 2031; UCO Bank's board approved raising up to $1 billion. Power Finance Corporation withdrew a three-year bond issue on high yields and instead accepted ₹2,500 crore at 15 years and 7.55% — a direct illustration of the RBI's hawkish minutes repricing corporate funding at the long end.

Business Standard, Chittorgarh

Two mainboard listings, opposite outcomes

Sunshine Pictures listed at ₹395.90 against an issue price of ₹360 (+9.97%), undershooting a grey-market premium that had implied roughly ₹410, on 105.81x subscription. Shankesh Jewellers listed at ₹103.30 against ₹93 (+11.08%), comfortably ahead of a roughly ₹2 grey-market premium, on 2.80x subscription — a reminder of how little predictive weight grey-market premium carries.

Chittorgarh, ipo-trend

Fertilisers rally, Hindustan Copper and IIFL fall

Fertiliser stocks rose sharply — FACT +14% to ₹894, Madras Fertilisers +10.7%, RCF +7.5%, Paradeep Phosphates +7.3%, National Fertilisers +7.1% — on reports that President Putin had signalled higher fertiliser supplies to India. Vodafone Idea rose 5% to ₹14.75 on reports that SBI-led consortium debt funding was near final. Hindustan Copper fell about 7% as its offer for sale opened at a ₹514 floor. IIFL Finance fell 4.46% after a ₹963.39 crore tax demand on subsidiary IIFL Home Finance; the company said it expects no material impact.

Business Standard, PL Capital, CNBC
