Tuesday, 25 August 2026

What moved overnight, and how it reaches Indian markets. Before the bell.

The overnight story

What happened. Asian markets showed a sharp divergence overnight: South Korea's Kospi and Taiwan's Taiex fell hard — Kospi down more than 4% intraday on top of a 3.12% fall on Monday, Taiex down 1.02% — while Hong Kong's Hang Seng (−0.15%) and Australia's ASX 200 (−0.10%) were essentially flat, pointing to a sector-specific move in memory semiconductors rather than broad regional risk aversion. Samsung Electronics fell 3.3%–3.8% and SK hynix 4.7%–5.0%, following a Monday session in which the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.76% even as the Dow rose 0.26%. Separately, the US Treasury announced its harshest sanctions yet on Iran — covering digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping, and naming more than sixty entities — yet Brent crude fell about 1.4% on the day, since the measures target financial flows rather than physical transit through the Strait of Hormuz and an uncertainty premium had already been priced in ahead of the announcement.

How this reaches India: Brent, back near $92.50 (roughly 39% above a year ago) after a modest Tuesday-morning bid, is the most direct channel: India imports the large majority of its crude, so a higher price widens the import bill and the trade deficit, raises input costs across transport, chemicals, fertiliser and aviation, and adds pressure on the rupee. That chain is already visible in the 5.3-percentage-point gap between July wholesale inflation (9.78%) and consumer inflation (4.45%) — an energy-cost shock sitting in producer prices that has not yet passed through to consumers, and a reason the RBI is holding a neutral rather than an easing stance. The same pressure interacts with USD/INR trading in the 95.77–95.82 zone, a level several analysts have flagged as one where the Reserve Bank has previously intervened to slow the pace of depreciation. On the rates side, US long-end Treasury yields — the 10-year at 4.709%, the 30-year having touched a nineteen-year high of 5.337% on 18 August — raise the discount rate applied to future earnings globally, compressing the valuations of long-duration growth names more than near-cash-flow value names, the same mechanism behind Monday's Dow-versus-Nasdaq split. And the Asian memory-chip selloff reaches Indian IT sentiment through an ownership correlation rather than an earnings one: the same global funds hold both memory-chip and Indian IT stocks even though India has no listed memory-chip manufacturing — which is why Nifty IT actually rose 0.21% on Monday even as the chip names fell.

The Nifty 50 closed Monday at 24,219.05, down 0.14%, and the Sensex at 77,369.11, down 0.22%, in a shallow, rotational decline. GIFT Nifty was quoted at 24,178.50 as of 08:46 IST, implying an opening gap of roughly ten points below Monday's close, though different providers showed conflicting signs within a 35-point band, leaving the pre-open signal weak in both directions.

Macro & Policy

RBI holds repo at 5.25%

Fourth consecutive hold, unanimous 6–0 vote, neutral stance retained. FY27 projections: 5.0% CPI (core 4.3%), 6.7% real GDP growth. Next MPC meeting 5–7 October.

RBI, 5 August MPC resolution; minutes released 19 August

CPI–WPI gap widens to 5.3 points

July CPI 4.45% against July WPI 9.78%, with mineral oils up 32.4% and crude petroleum/natural gas up 26.99% driving the wholesale figure — an energy-cost shock in producer prices not yet passed through to consumers.

MoSPI, July 2026 data

US long-end yields near multi-year highs

10-year Treasury at 4.709%; the 30-year touched 5.337% on 18 August, reported as a nineteen-year high. Higher long rates raise the discount rate applied to future earnings globally, compressing valuations of long-duration growth assets more than value assets.

Market-quoted yields; official Treasury curve could not be retrieved

Rupee: pace, not a defended level

Reported RBI practice has been to sell dollars through state-run banks to slow the pace of depreciation rather than defend a specific level. India's forex reserves stood at $716.9 billion, a six-month high.
