One-paragraph read

What happened. Monday was a shallow, rotational down day, not a risk-off day: Sensex −171.72 to 77,369.11 and Nifty −32.95 to 24,219.05, with 11 of 16 sectoral indices lower but Metal up 1.59% and BSE breadth only mildly negative at 2,041 advances to 2,393 declines. The tell is in the flows — both FIIs (+₹1,182cr) and DIIs (+₹2,493cr) were net cash buyers into a red tape, and DIIs have now bought for ten consecutive sessions. Against that, FIIs sit net short 2,19,383 index futures contracts with a long-short ratio of 9.90%. Cash buying against a heavy futures short is hedged accumulation, not conviction.

What matters today. Three things. First, today is the NSE monthly expiry for both Nifty and Bank Nifty (last Tuesday of the month under the post-2025 regime) — expiry-day mechanics, not fundamentals, will dominate the last hour, and August futures closed at a 30.95-point discount to spot. Second, USD/INR at ~95.77–95.82 is sitting exactly on the 95.80 level the market treats as the RBI's intervention zone, with the May record low a whisker away; that is the single highest-information price on the screen today. Third, the Asian tape is a semiconductor story rather than a macro one — Kospi down 3–4% on top of Monday's −3.12%, Taiex −1.02%, but Hang Seng −0.15% and ASX −0.10%. India's direct exposure to the memory-chip unwind is small; the transmission is sentiment into IT, not earnings.

Positioning bias. Neutral-to-defensive into a back-loaded week, with the risk skewed to the downside on crude rather than equities. The domestic calendar is empty today; the week's actual catalysts are all offshore and all after today — US GDP revision, durable goods and PCE together on Wednesday 18:00 IST, Nvidia after the US close (landing at Thursday's Indian open), and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote on Friday ~19:30 IST. Both central banks have stopped easing and are hinting the other way: three FOMC members dissented in July for a hike, and Governor Malhotra's minutes say policy "would need to respond" if input costs broaden. India's WPI at 9.78% against CPI at 4.45% is the evidence for that, and Brent up ~39% year-on-year is the reason. Expiry-day noise is not information; treat any move inside Kotak's 24,150–24,400 band as mechanical.

The gold number circulating is the wrong instrument. The widely repeated ~$4,714 figure is the December futures contract (prior close $4,709.90, now $4,719.34). Spot is roughly $4,645–4,660 — some $60–75 lower, a normal cost-of-carry gap at ~4.7% rates over four months. Publishing $4,714 as "gold spot" would be an error.

Why oil fell on the harshest Iran sanctions ever announced. Brent dropped ~1.4% on Monday into the news. Three mechanisms: the announcement removed the uncertainty premium built up ahead of it; the measures are financial rather than kinetic and do not physically threaten Hormuz transit; and US officials claimed Hormuz traffic is rising. But the supply picture remains impaired — Strait of Hormuz shipping volumes are running below 25% of pre-war levels, and Iran has threatened vessel seizures. The market is trading a single question: do the sanctions shorten the conflict (bearish crude, Hormuz reopens) or entrench it (bullish crude)? Tuesday's modest bid leans slightly to the latter.

USD/INR is the highest-information price today. Spot sits on 95.80, the level the market flags as the RBI's intervention zone, against a May record around 95.66–95.80. RBI is reported to be managing the pace of depreciation through state-run bank dollar sales rather than defending a line, and has raised nearly $73bn through measures introduced in June. Month-end importer demand and elevated crude are the pressure. The RBI reference rate could not be retrieved from rbi.org.in this morning; a TradingEconomics claim of a 99.82 all-time high in March does not reconcile with the documented May record and is not used.

Reserve Bank of India ✓P

Repo 5.25%, SDF 5.00%, MSF and Bank Rate 5.50%. Stance neutral, retained. Unanimous 6–0 hold on 5 August — a fourth consecutive hold. Governor Sanjay Malhotra. FY27 forecasts: CPI 5.0% (trimmed from 5.1%), core 4.3%; real GDP 6.7% (raised from 6.6%).

Next MPC 5–7 October. Minutes released 19 August: Malhotra says if food, fuel and input costs broaden, "monetary policy would need to respond" — but evidence of that "materialising was limited so far". The next move is more likely a hike than a cut.

The CPI–WPI wedge ✓P / S

July CPI 4.45% (PIB/MoSPI, primary) against July WPI 9.78% (single-sourced) — a 5.3 percentage point gap. WPI drivers on the new 2022-23 base: mineral oils +32.4%, crude petroleum and natural gas +26.99%, chemicals +13.12%.

This is an oil-and-input-cost shock sitting in the producer pipeline and not yet passed to consumers — precisely the "supply side pressures" the RBI flagged, and the mechanical reason a central bank with 4.45% consumer inflation is talking about tightening.

Operation Economic Outcast — announced yesterday C

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent launched the campaign on 24 August: five sectoral areas — digital assets, technology, gold, aviation, shipping — and 60+ entities, individuals and vessels designated. The teeth are secondary sanctions: third-country firms and financial institutions facilitating Iranian oil revenue face removal from the dollar system.

China is named; India is not named in any coverage located — but the architecture is generic and India has Gulf shipping and refining exposure. Iran's response: if the campaign continues, "not a single drop" of oil leaves Hormuz. The rial hit a record 2.02mn/USD Monday.

US–India tariffs — the 50% figure is stale C

An interim framework announced 6 February 2026 cut the US reciprocal tariff on Indian goods from 50% to 18%. Zero-duty access for gems and diamonds, pharmaceuticals, smartphones, tea, coffee and select agriculture; 18% on engineering goods, electronics, textiles, leather and chemicals. India protected dairy, rice and millets.

The comprehensive BTA remains unconcluded with no confirmed deadline.
