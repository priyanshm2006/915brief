The Sensex fell 2,000 points in twenty minutes yesterday, and then didn't

What happened. The Sensex traded above 77,100 all Thursday. Then, inside BSE's Closing Auction Session between roughly 3:15pm and 3:35pm, the indicative price collapsed to as low as 74,988 — a fall of more than 2,000 points, around 2.7% — and recovered almost all of it before the auction settled. The official close was 76,933.59, down 539.35 points or 0.70%. Reliance moved from ₹1,289 to ₹1,250 and back to ₹1,286 inside seven minutes. A 76,900 Sensex put went from ₹4.70 to ₹200 and expired at zero; a 77,000 call went from ₹327 to zero the moment the auction printed. This was the first monthly derivatives expiry since SEBI introduced the auction mechanism on 3 August. SEBI chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said there will be no changes to it.

How this reaches India: the channel here is domestic and mechanical rather than global, and it is the reason this matters more than the 539-point headline. A closing price is not just the last number on a screen — it is the settlement price for index futures and options, the mark for every index fund and ETF NAV struck that evening, the reference for margin calls, and the trigger level for resting stop-loss orders. Concentrating price discovery into a twenty-minute auction narrows the window in which all of that gets determined, and on an expiry day the auction is also absorbing hedge unwinds from positions that must be settled against the very price the auction is producing. That is a feedback loop, not a coincidence: expiry hedging demands liquidity from the auction at the same moment the auction is thin. The second-order channel runs offshore. Foreign passive money prices Indian index products partly on the reliability of the closing mark; a settlement print that can travel 2,000 points and back is a tracking-error and execution-risk input, and that feeds the risk premium attached to Indian index exposure — which is an FII flow channel, on a slower clock than a single session.

Thursday's benchmarks: Sensex 76,933.59 (−539.35, −0.70%), a second consecutive fall; Nifty 50 24,090.85 (−116.90, −0.48%). Midcaps (−0.10%) and smallcaps (−0.13%) were close to unchanged. GIFT Nifty was 24,257.00 at 08:45 IST, up 38.00 points (+0.16%) on its own prior close of 24,219.

The cash figure is small; the derivatives figure is not, and the two together say something the cash print alone hides. Foreign investors sold ₹298 crore of cash equities and simultaneously widened their net index-futures short by roughly 11,700 contracts, to about 1.98 lakh net short. A widening index short alongside a modest cash sale is a reduction in net directional exposure achieved mostly through the futures leg — the hedge is doing the work, not the sale. Domestic institutions bought ₹4,977 crore, which is the pattern that has held all month: on the NSE secondary-cash basis, August's domestic total is roughly nine times the foreign total. On the arithmetic: yesterday's figures reconcile exactly against the running month-to-date chain in both legs (5,792 − 298 = 5,494; 43,518 + 4,977 = 48,495), which is what establishes the direction of the foreign flow rather than merely corroborating its size. That check matters — this figure's sign has been published backwards by mainstream outlets four times in the last nine trading days.

The GIFT Nifty gap is mostly not news. GIFT Nifty at 24,257 sits about 166 points above Thursday's Nifty close of 24,090.85, and that whole gap gets quoted as an implied opening move most mornings. It isn't one. GIFT's own previous close was 24,219 — already roughly 128 points above spot, because it is a futures contract and carries cost of carry, consistent with the ~150-point premium the September future has been trading at. The overnight information is the +38.00 change, which implies an open near 24,129. Anyone reading the 166-point gap as an overnight signal is reading the interest-rate differential, not the news flow.

And on the US levels. Three providers gave three different S&P 500 closing levels for Thursday, spread about six points, with the Dow spread about 80. All agree on the percentage changes and the direction. We have published the percentages, which are cross-checked, and withheld the levels, which are not.

Macro & Policy

Warsh's Jackson Hole debut lands after you have gone home

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote today at 10:00 ET — 19:30 IST, more than four hours after the Indian close. The transmission timing matters more than the content preview: whatever repricing happens in Treasuries and the dollar tonight cannot reach Nifty until Monday's open, so today's Indian session trades the anticipation and Monday trades the event. The channel, if he moves rates, is the standard one — US real yields set the discount rate on long-duration cash flows worldwide, and Indian IT and growth names carry the most duration; a firmer dollar simultaneously pressures the rupee and the FII flow calculus. Investors have publicly said they want more clarity on how he reads current inflation dynamics than he has offered so far.

Speech time and role confirmed against the Federal Reserve Board calendar in prior runs · commentary via NPR

India's July industrial production is released today

MoSPI now publishes the Index of Industrial Production on the 28th of each month, having cut the release lag from 42 days to 28. June's IIP grew 7.3% with manufacturing at 7.8%. Today's print is the first hard read on the June-quarter industrial handoff before Monday's Q1 FY27 GDP release, and the two are mechanically linked — IIP feeds the industry component of the quarterly national accounts, so a July number that diverges sharply from the June-quarter trend shapes how Monday's GDP is read rather than merely preceding it.

Release schedule confirmed against PIB and the MoSPI Advance Release Calendar (primary)

Crude turned back up, reversing a five-session decline

Brent is quoted in a range of roughly $88.6 to $89.6 across providers, up between 0.8% and 2.0% depending on the snapshot; WTI is around $82 to $83. That ends a five-session slide that had taken Brent from above $92 to the mid-$86s. India imports the overwhelming majority of its crude, so the channel is direct and quantifiable: a sustained dollar-per-barrel move flows into the current-account deficit, into imported inflation through fuel and freight, and into the rupee via the oil import bill — which in turn feeds back into the RBI's reaction function. The disinflationary tailwind of the past week is not gone, but it stopped yesterday. Context worth holding: Gulf producers were reported on Thursday to be raising shipments through Hormuz despite the security situation, which cuts the other way, and Iran–Oman talks on the corridor continue.

Rates and the rupee going into the speech

The US 10-year Treasury yield is at 4.67%, essentially unchanged and a couple of basis points above where it sat mid-week. The rupee weakened on Thursday to roughly 95.54 per dollar, about 0.19% softer, with dollar strength reported as outweighing the support that cheaper oil had been providing — a support that has now partly reversed. Policy settings are unchanged on both sides: the Fed funds target is 3.50–3.75% following the 29 July FOMC, and the RBI repo rate is 5.25%.
