Wednesday, 19 August 2026

What moved overnight, and how it reaches Indian markets. Prior session: Tue 18 Aug.

The overnight story

Two things happened while India slept, and both arrived from outside. The first was a semiconductor unwind that started on Wall Street and landed hardest in Asia — the Kospi fell 6.39% and triggered a circuit breaker, the Nikkei fell 2.82%. The second was a bond and oil squeeze out of the US–Iran standoff: Brent has risen for four straight sessions to just under $92, and the US 30-year Treasury yield reached 5.31%, its highest level since 2007.

How this reaches India: rising US long-term yields raise the discount rate global investors apply to equities everywhere, hitting long-duration, high-growth names hardest — which is why chipmakers led the overnight fall rather than followed it. Brent near $92 reaches India through three channels at once: the current account, imported inflation, and the rupee, which touched a two-week low of 95.66 with the RBI intervening for an eighth consecutive session.

Indian markets closed lower on Tuesday for a third straight session on the Sensex and a sixth on the Nifty, with IT the heaviest drag. GIFT Nifty was trading around 24,206–24,240 ahead of the open, pointing to a flat-to-mildly-positive start.

On the Calendar

  • Tonight, 11:30 PM ISTFOMC minutes
    The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% on 29 July, a fifth consecutive hold on a 9–3 vote. All three dissenters wanted a 25bp increase — the first unified hawkish dissent since September 2016. Markets currently price roughly 31% odds of a September hike, down from about two-thirds right after the meeting.
  • Domestic policyRBI held at 5.25% on 5 August
    Neutral stance retained. FY27 GDP forecast raised to 6.7%; FY27 CPI forecast cut to 5.0%, with Q3 projected to peak at 5.9%. The central bank cited Middle East geopolitical tension as its reason for standing pat.
  • Domestic dataJuly GST and unemployment
    July GST collections were ₹2.11 lakh crore, up 15.4% year on year. July unemployment fell to 5.1% from 5.5%, a four-month low. ICRA estimates Q1 FY27 GDP growth at 6.4–6.6%.
