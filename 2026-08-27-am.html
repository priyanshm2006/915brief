Pre-Open Edition

Thursday, 27 August 2026

Two overnight channels, pointing opposite ways — and a GIFT Nifty print that is flatter than the headlines suggest.

Sticky US inflation against a fifth straight fall in crude

What happened. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge landed on Wednesday with core PCE at 3.3% year-on-year for July, unchanged from June and still 130 basis points above the 2% target, while headline PCE ran at 3.7% y/y (+0.2% m/m). Wall Street closed marginally lower on it — Dow −0.21%, S&P 500 −0.02%, Nasdaq Composite −0.08%. After the bell, Nvidia reported revenue of $96.2bn, up 106% y/y, data-centre revenue of $89.0bn and guided the current quarter to roughly $108bn — comfortably ahead — and the stock still fell about 1.3% on the first print before recovering close to 5% as management took questions. Separately, Brent fell for a fifth consecutive session as Iran–Oman talks raised the prospect of a navigational corridor through the Strait of Hormuz.

How this reaches India: two distinct channels, and today they pull in opposite directions. Channel one — rates. Core PCE stuck at 3.3% keeps a live hike option on the table for a Federal Reserve whose July decision already drew three dissents in favour of hiking, and whose Chairman, Kevin Warsh, delivers the Jackson Hole keynote tomorrow evening India time. That holds the long end of the US curve elevated, which compresses the yield pick-up an offshore investor earns for holding Indian assets and is transmitted here through FII allocation rather than through anything domestic — which is why Indian equities can fall on a US inflation print with no Indian data released at all. Channel two — oil. India imports roughly 85–88% of the crude it consumes, so the Brent strip is a direct input into the current-account deficit, into imported inflation via fuel and freight, and therefore into the rupee and into the RBI's room to hold at 5.25%. A sustained unwind of the Hormuz war premium is the single largest external variable that works in India's favour, and it is the reason India VIX has been grinding toward the low 10s rather than tracking global rate anxiety. The market's job at the open is to price which channel dominates.

Nifty closed Wednesday at 24,207.75, down 126.80 (−0.52%) and on the day's low, while the Sensex fell only 0.24% — a two-times divergence explained by the Sensex 30's heavier bank weighting, with Nifty Bank up 0.47% against Nifty IT down 1.47%. GIFT Nifty is quoted at 24,345.5, down 13.0 points (−0.05%) as of 08:00 IST, against its own prior close of 24,358.5 — see the Global Tape section for why the ~140-point gap to spot is not an implied gap-up.

The number that matters, and a warning about it. Foreign investors bought ₹502.6 crore of Indian cash equities on Wednesday while simultaneously holding a net short of roughly 1.86 lakh index-futures contracts. That combination is not a contradiction — it is cash exposure carried with an index hedge on top, which is what allocation into single stocks alongside a defensive view on the benchmark looks like. It also means the positive cash print overstates how constructive foreign positioning actually is.

Verification At least one major outlet this morning reports the same ₹502.63 crore figure as foreign investors having been net sellers. The direction is inverted. Three independent data platforms report it as a net buy, and the arithmetic settles it: August month-to-date foreign flows on the NSE secondary-cash basis were +₹5,289 crore through 25 August and are +₹5,792 crore through 26 August, a difference of +₹503 crore. Under a net sale the month-to-date chain does not reconcile. This is the fourth sign or date error we have caught in Indian flow data in nine trading days, across three different outlets. If you see this figure quoted as a sale today, it is wrong. Note also that the ₹5,792 crore month-to-date figure is NSE secondary-market cash only; NSDL's FPI series includes primary-market allotments and debt activity, measures a different universe, and is not comparable — we continue to publish no month-to-date figure on any other basis.

No Nifty or Bank Nifty put-call ratio, max pain or strike build-up is published here today, and that is deliberate rather than an omission. Our standing rule is that a derivatives figure requires both a stated expiry and a post-close timestamp before we will print it. Yesterday three separate providers, all labelling the same 1 September weekly contract, published put-call ratios spanning 0.79 to 1.17 — a 47% spread on a single nominal contract — and none carried a post-close stamp. Where peer values diverge that far with no primary source available, the honest conclusion is that the whole set is unusable, not that one of them is the outlier. The rollover figures above are from Systematix Institutional Research via a published F&O column and are attributed rather than independently confirmed; the mechanism they describe is that rollover ran above its three-month average while market-wide open interest contracted 4.3%, meaning fewer positions survived expiry but a larger share of the survivors were carried into September.

Read the GIFT Nifty gap correctly. GIFT Nifty at 24,345.5 sits roughly 138 points above Wednesday's Nifty spot close of 24,207.75, and that gap is routinely quoted as an implied opening move. Most of it is not a signal at all — it is futures basis, the cost of carry embedded in any futures contract against its underlying, and it is consistent with the September Nifty future's roughly 150-point premium to spot logged earlier this week. The number that carries overnight information is the change against GIFT's own prior close of 24,358.5, which is −13.0 points, or flat. That points to an open near Wednesday's close, not a 138-point gap-up. Two other levels are circulating this morning — one outlet quotes GIFT at 24,461 and another describes the same 24,345.5 level as "down 84 points" — and we cannot reconcile either against the 24,358.5 prior close that two independent sources agree on. Kospi's 1.37% is the outlier worth noticing: a Korean semiconductor complex responding to Nvidia's $108bn guide is the most direct read-across available from the AI capex cycle in Asia, and it has no equivalent in India. Hang Seng, Shanghai, Taiex, ASX and the European close could not be verified at time of writing and are not stated.

Macro & Policy

US core PCE holds at 3.3% — the hike option stays live

July core PCE rose 3.3% year-on-year, unchanged from June; headline PCE was 3.7% y/y and 0.2% month-on-month. Consensus had looked for core at 3.3%, so the core print met expectations while the headline ran hot. The significance is less the single reading than the absence of progress: with the target at 2%, a gauge that has stopped falling removes the argument for easing and leaves a Federal Reserve that already saw three dissents in favour of hiking at its 29 July decision with a genuinely open policy question.

Fed funds target 3.50–3.75% · confirmed against federalreserve.gov

Jackson Hole: Warsh keynote tomorrow, 19:30 IST

Chairman Kevin Warsh delivers the Jackson Hole keynote on Friday 28 August at 10:00 ET, which is 19:30 IST — after Indian markets close, meaning any repricing reaches Nifty at Monday's open rather than Friday's. The Kansas City Fed publishes only dates and a theme and posts no agenda; the time is confirmed against the Federal Reserve Board's own August calendar. Calendar pages still naming Powell as keynote speaker are stale.

Crude: the war premium is unwinding, not gone

Brent has now fallen five consecutive sessions on reports of an Iran–Oman framework for a navigational corridor through the Strait of Hormuz. Sources this morning quote the front-month contract between $86.40 and $87.20, a spread we cannot resolve, so we state the range. The India-relevant point is that Brent remains up roughly 28% year-on-year: most of the conflict premium is still in the price, and what has moved is the probability of the tail, not the level. Note that quotes for a "Brent September contract" are appearing in some coverage; that contract has expired.

Contested level · direction cross-checked

India's own calendar: IIP tomorrow, GDP Monday

India industrial production is released Friday 28 August and Q1 FY27 GDP on Monday 31 August, both per MoSPI's official Advance Release Calendar — not Thursday, as at least one third-party aggregator states. Forecasts for the GDP print are unusually widely dispersed, from around 7.0% in a published survey to roughly 8.0% at SBI Research, against a 7.8% year-ago base. A dispersion that wide is itself the information: it means the print has more capacity to move the rupee and the rates curve than a typical quarter.
