The one-paragraph read

What happened. Expiry day delivered a reversal, not a drift. The Nifty bottomed at 24,115.45 in the morning and closed at 24,334.55, up 115.50 points (+0.48%) — a 219-point round trip, with roughly 75 of the closing points supplied by the new Closing Auction Session mechanism. The Sensex added 286.98 to 77,656.09. It was one up session, snapping a single down day, not the resumption of a trend. Two things sit awkwardly under that green print: breadth was negative — declines outnumbered advances on both exchanges despite the index gain — and Bank Nifty went nowhere (57,514.20, −11.75). The genuine strength was elsewhere: the Nifty Midcap 100 closed at a record 64,162.9 (+0.54%), while smallcaps slipped 0.10%. Pharma, consumer durables and PSU banks led; energy and metals lagged.

What matters today. The overnight setup is the cleanest India-positive configuration in weeks, and it is all one trade unwinding. Washington's Iran sanctions package — trailed as "economic D-Day" — landed on 25 August without secondary sanctions on Iran's trading partners, without designating the Chinese banks that clear roughly 90% of Iran's oil exports, and without effective dates; Iran and Oman then opened talks on a temporary Hormuz maritime corridor. Brent is down a third straight session to ~$86.28 (−2.6%), the Murban premium to Brent has roughly halved in two days, the US 10-year has fallen ~6.5bp to ~4.63% off a 20-month high, and the dollar index is near three-month lows at ~98.91. Cheaper crude, lower yields and a soft dollar is the textbook transmission set for an oil-importing market. GIFT Nifty at 24,559.50 (+87.80, ~08:05 IST) pointed to an open near 24,420 once the ~137-point futures basis is stripped out — not the +225 the raw gap to spot implies.

Positioning bias. Constructive into the open, but the payoff is asymmetric against tonight's calendar and the conviction behind the move is thin. Three qualifiers. First, FIIs remain heavily net short index futures — −1,84,227 contracts, longs just 10.29% of open interest — so a crude-led relief rally has short-covering fuel but no confirmed cash-market conviction underneath it; the 25 August FII cash buy of ₹1,593.53 crore is real but small, and it currently rests on three light-tier aggregators while the heavier providers have not yet published. Second, US Q2 GDP, July PCE and durable goods all land at 18:00 IST, and Nvidia reports at ~01:30 IST — the entire risk budget for this move is spent after the Indian close. Third, and least priced: WPI at 9.78% against CPI at 4.45% is a 533bp divergence, and the August MPC minutes had the Deputy Governor saying explicitly that "a case for a hike may emerge." The domestic rate risk is a hike, not a cut, and cheaper crude is the only thing currently arguing against it. Buying the oil-relief trade is buying a diplomacy headline, not a resolution.

Transposition ruled out by arithmetic — how
Through 24 Aug the MTD anchors were FII +₹3,695.94 cr and DII +₹36,862.75 cr (independently confirmed on Kotak Neo and StockEdge). Adding the 25 Aug figures: 3,695.94 + 1,593.53 = 5,289.47 and 36,862.75 + 230.26 = 37,093.01 — both match NiftyTrader's separately published August MTD (+5,289 / +37,093) to rounding. Under a transposition neither leg reconciles. The orientation is therefore proved, not merely asserted. What is not proved is the absolute level: Kotak Neo, StockEdge, 5paisa, ScanX, Groww, OptionX, Craytheon and Traderscockpit had all not published a 25 August row at time of writing, so this rests on three lighter-tier providers plus the reconciliation above.

Known flow-data traps, live today

  • Business Today — reports 25 Aug FPIs as "net sellers of ₹1,181.66 cr." Wrong date and wrong sign; those are the 24 Aug net-buy figures.
  • Upstox — off-by-one date label throughout. Its "25-08-2026" row is the 24 Aug data; its "24-08-2026" row (FII −542.71) is the 21 Aug data. Anything sourced from Upstox today is one session stale under a current-date label.
  • IIFL / indiainfoline — publishes FII +₹24,167.07 cr / DII +₹27,886.68 cr against 25 Aug. This is a different universe (its DII gross buy is under half StockEdge's NSE-cash figure) and reconciles with nothing. Do not mix.
  • Groww — serves a rolling ten-session window (11–24 Aug) that reads like a month-to-date. It is not, and must not be differenced against an MTD figure.
  • NSDL FPI data — includes primary-market/IPO allotments and the debt segment. Not comparable to NSE secondary cash. This is the unresolved conflict logged on 25 August, and it remains open.

Macro & policy

RBI — the risk is a hike, not a cut
Repo 5.25%, stance neutral, held 5 August (fourth consecutive hold, 6–0). The 19 August minutes read hawkish: Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta — "given that headline inflation is projected to peak to a level as high as 5.9% in Q3 2026-27, a case for a hike may emerge during the course of the year." Governor Malhotra — "any evidence of these risks materialising may need policy tightening." No member voted to hike. Next MPC 5–7 October.
FY27 GDP projection 6.7%; FY27 CPI 5.0%, with Q3 at 5.9%. 7-day VRRR today absorbing ₹2.5 trn; system surplus ~₹3.5 trn, WACR 5.19%.
The CPI–WPI divergence is the underpriced domestic risk
July CPI 4.45% against July WPI 9.78% — a 533bp gap, driven by energy pass-through from the Iran war and a 95-plus rupee. WPI leads. This is why the minutes read as they do, and why a headline "inflation is contained" framing off CPI alone is wrong.
IIP (Jun) +7.3%, a 23-month high. GST (Jul) ₹2.11 lakh cr, +15.4% y/y. Trade deficit (Jul) $31.98 bn, a six-month high, with exports +19.63%. FX reserves $716.907 bn (w/e 14 Aug).
Fed — a hike debate, not a cut debate
Target 3.50–3.75%, held 28–29 July on a 9–3 vote — and all three dissents (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) wanted a 25bp HIKE. Chair is Kevin Warsh. Market-implied odds of a September hike have collapsed from ~65% (5 Aug) to ~30–34% now on the payrolls miss and softer retail sales. Next FOMC 15–16 September.
Goldman calls current pricing "too hawkish," expecting 3.50–3.75% through end-2026. One aggregator (rateprobability.com) shows a −50.6bp cumulative figure internally inconsistent with its own 34.4% hike probability — discarded.
US–India tariffs — position now confirmed against the Federal Register
Current treatment: MFN base duty plus a 10% Section 301 forced-labour additional duty, uncapped. India sits in the 10% tier (not the 12.5% default) because it amended its Foreign Trade Policy to prohibit forced-labour imports. India does not get the net-of-MFN cap given to the EU, Taiwan, Japan, Korea and Switzerland — so the duty is genuinely additive.
Chain: SCOTUS struck down the IEEPA tariffs 6–3 on 20 Feb 2026, voiding the negotiated 18% reciprocal rate; a 10% Section 122 surcharge ran from 24 Feb and sunset by operation of law on 24 July; the Section 301 action took effect the same instant. Annex I–II exclude 2,120 tariff lines; Section 232 sectoral tariffs (steel, aluminium, autos) remain separate and unaffected. Upgraded from "probable" to confirmed — Federal Register 2026-15181, USTR/EY, Global Trade Alert.
Iran — the whole overnight move, and it is diplomacy not resolution
Treasury Secretary Bessent's "Operation Economic Outcast" (25 Aug) sanctioned 60 individuals and entities across digital assets, gold, technology, aviation and shipping. Markets read it as underwhelming: no secondary sanctions on Iran's trading partners, no designation of the Chinese banks handling ~90% of Iran's oil exports, no implementation deadline. Iran separately reopened Hormuz corridor talks with Oman.
The cleanest read on the unwind: the Murban premium to Brent roughly halved Monday to Tuesday from ~$11. Brent is still +27.9% y/y — most of the war premium is intact, and reversal risk on any Hormuz incident is high. A US–Iran war has been running ~6 months; any commentary off a pre-2026 template is wrong.
